CHAPTER 04 — HERO
Purpose

The Hero is not an introduction.

It is the opening scene of the entire experience.

Within the first five seconds the visitor should immediately understand that this portfolio is fundamentally different from a traditional developer website.

The Hero is responsible for creating curiosity.

It should never attempt to explain everything.

Its job is to make the visitor continue scrolling.

User Psychology

When the Hero appears, the visitor should subconsciously experience the following sequence.

Curiosity

↓

Visual Surprise

↓

Exploration

↓

Scroll

↓

Engagement

The Hero succeeds only if the visitor naturally wants to discover what comes next.

Story Role

The Hero introduces the person.

It does not introduce the projects.

It does not introduce services.

It introduces the creator behind everything.

The character becomes the visual representation of the developer.

The visitor begins connecting with a person instead of a résumé.

Layout Philosophy

The Hero occupies the entire screen.

Nothing should compete with the character.

The composition follows one dominant focal point.

Background

↓

Character

↓

Headline

↓

Supporting Text

↓

Primary CTA

Every additional element reduces focus.

Only essential information belongs here.

Character

The character is the heart of the Hero.

It is not decoration.

It is the identity of the portfolio.

The character should appear calm, confident, and approachable.

The character never behaves like a game character.

No exaggerated expressions.

No unnecessary movements.

No cartoon-like actions.

Every motion should feel deliberate.

Character Animation

The animation is created from image frames rather than a real-time 3D engine.

This ensures visual consistency while maintaining high performance.

The visitor should never realize the sequence is frame-based.

The animation should feel like a premium cinematic render.

Scroll Behavior

The Hero uses scroll as its timeline.

Scrolling should control the progression naturally.

The visitor should never feel that scrolling has been hijacked.

The Hero remains pinned while the animation completes.

Once the sequence finishes, the page transitions seamlessly into the About section.

Background

The Hero background establishes the visual identity of the entire portfolio.

It uses the global gradient defined in the Design System.

The background should contain subtle environmental lighting.

Never use distracting objects.

Never place decorative graphics behind the character.

Negative space is intentional.

Typography

Typography must remain secondary to the character.

The heading should communicate confidence without occupying excessive space.

Large.

Simple.

Readable.

Timeless.

The supporting text explains the creator's philosophy in one concise statement.

Avoid paragraphs.

Primary Heading

The heading follows this hierarchy.

Small introductory phrase.

↓

Primary name.

↓

Optional highlighted keyword.

The visitor's eye should naturally stop on the developer's name.

Background Typography

Very large low-opacity typography may exist behind the character.

Its purpose is environmental depth.

It should never compete with foreground content.

Opacity should remain extremely low.

The user should notice it only after a few seconds.

Call To Action

Only essential actions belong inside the Hero.

Recommended actions.

Resume

Contact

Projects

No more than two primary actions.

Too many buttons weaken focus.

Glass Components

Buttons follow the global glass specification.

The Hero should not introduce a unique component style.

Consistency is more valuable than experimentation.

Camera Language

Although the animation is frame-based, it should simulate professional cinematography.

The camera should feel stable.

No rapid movements.

No dramatic zooms.

The experience should resemble a premium product advertisement.

Lighting

Studio lighting remains soft.

Primary light originates from the upper left.

Secondary bounce light fills darker areas.

Reflections should enhance facial definition without becoming dramatic.

Ambient Motion

Environmental movement remains subtle.

Allowed examples.

• Slow gradient drift.

• Tiny floating dust particles.

• Glass reflections.

• Gentle atmospheric light.

Everything moves slowly enough that visitors feel the environment rather than consciously observing animation.

Scroll Indicator

A minimal scroll indicator may appear near the bottom of the Hero.

It should disappear naturally after scrolling begins.

Never blink.

Never bounce aggressively.

Transition Into About

This is one of the most important transitions in the portfolio.

The Hero must never abruptly end.

Instead, it transforms into the About section.

The character gradually moves toward its final resting position.

The environment remains visually connected.

The visitor should perceive one continuous scene instead of two independent sections.

This transition is considered one of the portfolio's signature moments.

Performance Rules

The Hero is allowed to consume more resources than ordinary sections because it represents the strongest first impression.

However, interaction must remain smooth.

Target frame rate:

Desktop: 60 FPS

Minimum acceptable: 50 FPS

If performance drops, visual complexity should be reduced before responsiveness.

Accessibility

If reduced motion is enabled.

Replace scroll-scrub animation with a static hero.

Keep typography.

Keep layout.

Keep hierarchy.

Remove cinematic transitions.

The portfolio must remain equally understandable without animation.

Success Criteria

The Hero succeeds when visitors immediately think:

"This doesn't feel like a normal portfolio."

and then continue scrolling without hesitation.

If the Hero creates curiosity without overwhelming the visitor, it has achieved its purpose.
