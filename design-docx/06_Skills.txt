# CHAPTER 06 — SKILLS

---

# Purpose

The Skills section is not a technology list.

It is visual evidence of capability.

Most portfolios display logos inside grids.

This portfolio communicates movement, growth, confidence, and technical depth.

The visitor should immediately understand that these technologies are tools used to build products—not decorations added to fill space.

The section answers one question.

> **"What is this developer capable of building?"**

---

# Story Role

Within the portfolio journey, Skills acts as the bridge between identity and proof.

The narrative becomes:

```text
Who I Am

↓

How I Think

↓

What I Can Build

↓

Projects
```

Without this bridge, Projects feel disconnected.

---

# Emotional Goal

Visitors should feel:

Confidence

↓

Breadth

↓

Professionalism

↓

Curiosity

↓

"I want to see the projects."

The Skills section should naturally create anticipation for the Projects section.

---

# Design Philosophy

Technology is always moving.

The interface should communicate this visually.

Instead of static icons sitting in rows, technologies continuously travel through the scene.

The motion represents continuous learning.

The visitor should feel that the developer's skillset is alive and evolving.

---

# Layout

The section follows a vertical structure.

```text
Section Heading

↓

Short Description

↓

Moving Technology Environment

↓

Category Summary

↓

Transition Into Projects
```

The technology environment occupies most of the section.

---

# Technology Environment

The primary visual element consists of two continuous horizontal technology strips.

These strips create depth and movement without distracting from the content.

The strips are not carousels.

They are infinite loops.

The movement should never visibly restart.

---

# Strip System

Upper Strip

Direction:

Left → Right

Lower Strip

Direction:

Right → Left

Because both strips move in opposite directions, the interface feels balanced and dynamic.

---

# Motion Behaviour

Movement speed remains slow.

Average speed:

25–35 pixels per second.

The objective is not to showcase animation.

The objective is to create an ambient technological environment.

---

# Cursor Interaction

Mouse movement influences the strips slightly.

When the cursor approaches either strip:

Movement gradually slows.

Icons gently separate.

Nearby technologies receive a subtle emphasis.

The effect resembles magnetic influence rather than hover animation.

Once the cursor leaves, motion smoothly returns to its original speed.

Nothing should stop abruptly.

---

# Infinite Loop

The strips must loop seamlessly.

The visitor should never discover where the animation begins or ends.

Looping should feel mathematically perfect.

---

# Icon Style

Icons must use official branding.

Flat logos.

High quality.

Consistent visual size.

No colored backgrounds.

No inconsistent padding.

No circular badges unless provided by the official brand.

---

# Icon Size

Desktop:

48–56px

Tablet:

44px

Mobile:

36–40px

Visual weight must remain consistent even when logo proportions differ.

---

# Spacing

Every logo should maintain identical spacing.

Recommended gap:

40–56px

The eye should perceive rhythm.

Not randomness.

---

# Categories

Technologies should be mentally grouped even if the strips appear continuous.

Suggested groups include:

Programming Languages

Frontend

Backend

Databases

Cloud

AI

Tools

DevOps

Design

Version Control

Each technology belongs to only one category.

---

# Glass Interaction

The strips move above a subtle glass environment.

The glass should never overpower the logos.

Its purpose is to create depth.

Not decoration.

---

# Lighting

A soft diagonal reflection occasionally passes across the strips.

Duration:

6–8 seconds.

Opacity:

Very low.

The visitor should perceive premium materials rather than animation.

---

# Ambient Effects

Allowed:

Subtle floating particles.

Very soft bloom.

Environmental lighting.

Background gradient movement.

Not allowed:

Fire.

Electricity.

Neon streaks.

RGB trails.

Spark explosions.

Gaming effects.

---

# Typography

Typography remains minimal.

The section title introduces the capability.

The supporting description explains the philosophy behind continuous learning.

Avoid listing every technology in text.

The logos already communicate that information.

---

# Performance

The animation must remain lightweight.

The strips should rely on GPU-friendly transforms.

Avoid unnecessary re-renders.

Target:

60 FPS desktop.

Stable mobile performance.

Smoothness is more important than visual complexity.

---

# Accessibility

If reduced motion is enabled:

Pause strip movement.

Display technologies in a clean static layout.

Maintain spacing.

Maintain hierarchy.

Remove only motion.

Never remove information.

---

# Transition Into Projects

This transition is one of the most important moments in the portfolio.

The technology strips should not simply disappear.

Instead, their movement gradually slows.

The environment becomes calmer.

The visitor subconsciously understands that the technologies are about to become real products.

The transition should feel like moving from preparation into execution.

Projects are the natural consequence of the skills shown here.

---

# Success Criteria

The Skills section succeeds when visitors think:

> "This developer has a broad and organized technical foundation."

Immediately afterward, they should want to answer the next question:

> "What have they actually built?"

If the visitor reaches the Projects section with genuine curiosity rather than obligation, the Skills section has fulfilled its purpose.
